Shared player-language guide for description generation and fidelity review.
These are phrasing options. Follow the example's description policy: exact
paraphrases preserve every expressed constraint; broad descriptions may omit
details and summarize a reference card as one plausible completion. In either
case, claims the description DOES make must be compatible with the card.
Never add facts merely from a famous card name or a glossary example. The
detail-preservation cautions below apply to details the policy asks to express;
they do not require broad descriptions to list every cost and restriction.

Use varied voices: some requests should use plain beginner language, others
familiar player shorthand. Prefer natural phrasing over mechanically substituting
every available synonym. Keep complete descriptions readable; casual_complete
and mechanics can use more shorthand. A glossary term need not appear in every
description. Sparse requests must stay sparse.

Planeswalkers:
- "plus", "plus ability", "tick up", and "uptick" can describe a positive
  loyalty ability; "minus", "tick down", and "downtick" a negative one.
- "ult" or "ultimate" can describe a planeswalker's large payoff ability.
  It is an informal label, not an Oracle keyword or a loyalty cost. Not every
  planeswalker has an ultimate, and not every minus ability is an ultimate.
- When exact expressed constraints include a numeric loyalty cost, retain it:
  "plus one: make a ... token" or "its -7 ult ...". Do not replace -7 with
  just "ultimate" in a complete-mechanics example. Preserve starting loyalty
  separately. Never introduce the nonexistent {L} symbol.

Zones, events, and objects:
- "ETB", "when it comes into play", and "when it enters" can express an
  enters-the-battlefield trigger; "in play" can mean on the battlefield.
  An enters trigger is different from a cast trigger.
- "deck" can mean library in phrases such as "top card of their deck";
  do not confuse this zone with deck construction. "yard" or "grave" can
  mean graveyard. Preserve whose zone it is.
- "remove from the game" is historical wording for exile. Destroy, sacrifice,
  discard, mill, and exile are different actions, not general removal synonyms.
- "dies" describes a creature going from the battlefield to a graveyard;
  it does not mean any departure. "sac" can mean sacrifice. Keep who sacrifices
  what, and whether sacrifice is a cost or an effect.
- "flyer" can mean a creature with flying. "body" can informally mean a
  creature; retain required card type/subtype words and exact P/T where given.

Common actions, with the details that must survive:
- "bounce" for returning to hand: retain the object, target restrictions,
  and whose hand. It is not returning to the top of a library.
- "blink" or "flicker" for exile and return: explicitly preserve when it
  returns, which zone it returns to, and under whose control.
- "loot" for draw then discard; "rummage" for discard then draw. Preserve
  both counts, order, optionality, and whether discard is a payment or effect.
- "mill" for putting top library cards into the graveyard. Preserve the
  player and count. Milling is neither drawing nor exiling those cards.
- "tutor" for a search: still state the searched zone, card restrictions,
  destination, reveal requirements, and shuffle instruction if supplied.
- "reanimate" for a graveyard-to-battlefield return: preserve eligible cards,
  the graveyard's owner, controller of the returned card, and any restrictions.
- "pump" or "buff" for a stat boost: keep the exact change, recipients,
  duration, and whether it uses counters or a continuous effect.
- "drain" for life loss plus life gain: specify who loses/gains and how much.
  Life loss and damage are different; lifelink is not a generic drain effect.
- "counter" a spell/ability differs from putting counters on an object or
  player. Preserve the counter kind and recipient.

More player vocabulary:
- "cantrip" adds card draw to another effect; preserve the draw's timing if
  specified. It does not mean scry, investigate, or a cast trigger by default.
- "wheel" describes replacing hands with new cards. Counts, affected players,
  and discard versus shuffling hands away vary; do not assume everyone draws seven.
- "impulse draw" or "impulsive draw" exiles cards with permission to use
  them. Keep the duration and play/cast distinction when specified; this differs
  from looking at several cards and selecting one for your hand.
- "edict" means a forced-sacrifice effect. The affected player normally
  chooses the sacrifice; targeting that player is not targeting their creature.
- "wrath", "sweeper", or "board wipe" means broad removal. They do not
  inherently specify destroy versus exile or which permanent types are affected.
- "anthem" means a team boost; "lord" often means a creature buffing a
  creature type. Preserve other-versus-all, controller scope, and the actual boost.
- "ping" often means one damage; "burn" means direct damage. A "pinger"
  does not alone specify its activation cost or whether players can be targeted.
- "fight" involves creatures dealing power-based damage to each other;
  "bite" is one-way power-based damage. Neither is combat damage.
- "fog" refers to combat-damage prevention; preserve who is protected and
  for how long when those details are requested.
- "firebreathing" describes an activated power boost; costs and amounts vary.
- "mana dork" describes a mana-producing creature; "mana rock" a
  mana-producing artifact. Neither fixes the mana amount, color, or activation cost.
- "ramp" increases mana availability; "fixing" helps access colors. Searching
  for lands into hand, putting them onto the battlefield, and making Treasure
  are different implementations, not exact synonyms.
- "sac outlet" is a way to sacrifice something for a benefit. "Aristocrats"
  describes sacrifice/death synergies, not a particular ability or creature type.
- "threaten" commonly describes temporary control; "steal" can be broader.
  Do not silently add untapping, haste, or a duration absent from the card.
- "clone" can describe a copy effect. Copying a permanent, creating a token
  copy, and copying a spell are different; preserve exceptions and duration.
- "ETBT" means enters tapped, not an enters trigger. "freeze" or "tap down"
  can describe tap/untap suppression; use the card's duration, not assumed counters.
- "vanilla" explicitly means no abilities. "French vanilla" means only
  keyword abilities. Do not use either just because a request omits its abilities.
- A design "bear" often means a 2/2 for two mana, not the Bear subtype.
  "Evasive" need not mean flying. "Voltron", "tokens", and "go wide"
  describe strategies; they are not precise rules text.
- "Esper" means white/blue/black; "Grixis" blue/black/red; "Jund"
  black/red/green; "Naya" red/green/white; "Bant" green/white/blue.
  Color shorthand does not specify exact costs or imply a creature subtype.

Ambiguities and limits:
- "play from the top of their deck" preserves a play permission, not drawing,
  searching, copying, or putting a card into hand. Play can include lands;
  cast cannot. Do not add free casting, looking, revealing, extra land plays,
  or flexible mana spending unless supplied.
- "kill", "removal", "board wipe", "ramp", "steal", and "double" alone
  do not identify a complete mechanic. Keep the action, scope, timing, and
  limitations explicit when a complete effect is requested.
- "small", "midsize", "big", or "cheap" are qualitative, not exact stats
  or mana costs. A "three-drop" alone does not specify {2}{R}. Keep exact
  mana costs in braced notation and exact stats as P/T when supplied.
- Preserve may versus must, target versus each, owner versus controller,
  you versus opponents, duration, and all numeric amounts. Slang does not
  excuse missing constraints. Plain language is preferable to ambiguous slang.
